Your foundation is the one part of your home you never see — until something goes wrong. And more often than not, when something does go wrong, poor drainage is the root cause.

## Why Water Is Your Foundation's Biggest Enemy

Soil expands when it's saturated with water and contracts as it dries out. When drainage around your home is poor, the soil next to your foundation goes through this expansion-and-contraction cycle over and over, especially through a Texas summer of heavy storms followed by weeks of drought. That constant movement puts pressure on your foundation and, over time, can lead to:

- Cracks in the foundation walls or slab
- Doors and windows that stick or won't close properly
- Uneven or sloping floors
- Gaps between walls and ceilings
- Water intrusion into crawl spaces or basements

## The Warning Signs to Watch For

Most foundation damage doesn't happen overnight — it builds slowly. Keep an eye out for:

- Standing water near your foundation more than 24 hours after rain
- Visible cracks in exterior brick or siding near the base of the house
- Musty odors or moisture in crawl spaces
- New cracks in interior drywall, especially near corners of doors and windows

If you notice any of these, it's worth having your drainage — not just your foundation — inspected. Very often the foundation is the symptom, and the drainage is the cause. ## How Proper Drainage Protects Your Foundation

The fix isn't always a foundation repair. In many cases, redirecting water away from the house solves the problem before it starts. That typically means:

**Grading.** The soil around your home should slope away from the foundation, not toward it. Even a small amount of negative grading can funnel thousands of gallons of water toward your foundation over a rainy season.

**French drains.** A French drain is a gravel-filled trench with a perforated pipe that intercepts groundwater and carries it away from vulnerable areas before it can pool. See our full [French Drains service](/services) for details on installation.

**Underground drainage systems.** For homes with more significant water issues — like a downspout that dumps water right next to the foundation — an [underground drainage system](/services) routes that water through buried pipe to a safe discharge point well away from the house.

**Gutter maintenance.** Clogged or poorly positioned gutters are one of the most common (and most fixable) causes of foundation-adjacent water pooling.

## When to Call a Professional

If you're seeing pooling water, cracks, or other warning signs, it's worth getting a drainage assessment before the problem grows into a foundation repair. A properly designed drainage system is almost always less expensive than fixing foundation damage after the fact.
