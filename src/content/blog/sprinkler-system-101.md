A well-designed sprinkler system is one of the best investments you can make in your lawn — but only if it's set up and maintained correctly. Here's what every homeowner should know.

## Why Sprinkler Systems Beat Hand Watering

Hand watering with a hose almost always leads to uneven coverage — some spots get soaked while others dry out, leading to patchy grass. A properly zoned sprinkler system delivers consistent, even coverage across your entire yard, on a schedule that matches what your lawn actually needs. ## Getting the Zones Right

Not all parts of your yard need the same amount of water. Sunny, exposed areas dry out faster than shaded spots. Slopes drain differently than flat ground. A good sprinkler design separates your yard into zones based on sun exposure, slope, and plant type, so each area gets exactly the water it needs — no more, no less.

## How Much and How Often

A common mistake is watering a little bit every day. That encourages shallow root growth and makes your lawn more vulnerable to heat and drought stress. Most Texas lawns do better with deeper, less frequent watering — generally two to three times per week, early in the morning, so water has time to soak in before the heat of the day causes evaporation.

## Signs Your System Needs a Tune-Up

- Brown or patchy spots in an otherwise green lawn (often a sign of a broken or misaligned head)
- Water pooling on sidewalks or driveways (overspray or a broken line)
- A noticeable spike in your water bill with no change in your schedule
- Heads that don't pop up fully or don't rotate through their full arc

Sprinkler heads shift over time from mowing, foot traffic, and seasonal ground movement. An annual inspection and tune-up catches these issues before they turn into a wasted water bill or a dead patch of lawn.

## Seasonal Adjustments Matter

Your sprinkler schedule shouldn't be "set it and forget it." Watering needs change dramatically between a mild spring and a 100-degree August, and running a summer schedule into the fall wastes water and money. Adjusting your controller seasonally — or installing a smart controller that adjusts automatically — is one of the simplest ways to cut water waste without sacrificing lawn health.

## Sprinklers and Drip Systems Work Together

For flower beds, shrubs, and garden areas, [drip irrigation](/services) is often a better fit than spray heads — it delivers water directly to the root zone with minimal evaporation or runoff.
